Vivid's Apple TV Home uses a discovery spotlight above collection-based media rows on the selected app theme described in [App Design](app-design.md#appearance). Movies, Series and For You each keep their own page and sub-tab selection.

## Home and discovery spotlight

- The spotlight contains at most ten unique items, drawn in turn from up to three selected Home rows. New selections start with the first three populated rows. Configure these separately from row visibility in Settings → General → Spotlight.
- The spotlight uses separate rounded cards with neighbouring cards peeking in at both sides. Each slide is a native focus target in a horizontal scroll view, with a fixed decorative gradient outline and no focus enlargement, parallax or colour glow. The system owns directional focus and scrolling. Automatic rotation remains on a six-second timer; each automatic advance uses a 0.45-second transition, disabled by Reduce Motion. Its layout height remains 580 points. Logo and metadata stay together on the left.
- Each card retains the 580-point height and is 120 points narrower than the available viewport, with 22-point gaps. Server backdrops fill the card without stretching, using the existing face/head, person and saliency detection to guide cropping. Wide framing can still trim the image, especially with multiple subjects; detection cannot guarantee perfect composition. There is no spotlight TMDB lookup or blurred neighbouring-image overlay. Configured TMDb access supports trailers and Emby IMDb-to-TMDb identity resolution; the spotlight makes no TMDb requests. Movie runtime is omitted from spotlight metadata. The owner approved the sliding card design on Apple TV. The owner accepted the native focus conversion on Apple TV at `00177cf`.

- Moving Up from the first row to the eagerly mounted spotlight uses native directional focus and scrolling, without a forced focus handoff or manual scroll animation. Explicit focus requests remain for tab-bar entry and detail returns. While a spotlight exists, the first row does not keep a high-priority default-focus preference. Remembered horizontal card selections do not invalidate the whole Home feed. The owner reported smoother navigation after the native `.card` conversion and accepted the resulting Home behaviour on Apple TV.

## Studios & Networks

- Home pins a Studios & Networks row directly under the spotlight, without a heading. With the spotlight turned off, the row tops Home and takes focus first. Six logo tiles span the spotlight card's width with 30-point gaps, and the row scrolls sideways to the rest, up to 20. Every tile is built up front, so focus can return to one scrolled out of view. Tile size is fixed. Without a signed-in account and profile, or when no brand has enough matches, the row is absent and the spotlight hands focus straight to the first row as before.
- Down from the spotlight lands on the row, returning to the last tile used; Up, Down and swipes out of the row use native focus, as between other Home rows: Down lands on the card below in the first media row and Up returns to the spotlight. With the spotlight turned off, Up from the row returns to the top menu. Returning from a brand page puts focus back on the tile that opened it. An earlier manual Down handler raced native focus on Siri Remote swipes and pulled focus to the remembered Continue Watching card, so the row no longer claims focus for vertical moves. It is not a Home section: it is excluded from the six-row limit, the Home Sections editor, Spotlight sources and Top Shelf.
- The catalogue covers Netflix, HBO, Apple TV, Disney+, Prime Video, Hulu, Peacock, Paramount+, Stan, BritBox, AMC, FX, Pixar, Marvel Studios, A24, Lucasfilm, DreamWorks, Studio Ghibli, Sony Pictures, Warner Bros., Universal, Walt Disney Pictures, DC and 20th Century Studios. TMDb supplies each logo, shown as a white silhouette; the lettering of Marvel's block and the Warner Bros. and DC shields is knocked out, the near-square shields and the 20th Century Studios logo may use a little more of the tile's height so they don't look smaller than the wide wordmarks, and Apple TV uses a wordmark in the system typeface because its TMDb logo carries the Apple mark. The wordmark is drawn once and scaled like the logos, so its letter spacing is the same on every tile size. Sony Pictures' one-line logo is redrawn from the full-size TMDb image with SONY stacked above PICTURES (scaled to the same width) beside its emblem, so it reads at tile size; if the logo ever stops splitting into an emblem and two words, it's shown unchanged. Saved results that predate a newly added brand are matched again on the next load instead of waiting for the daily refresh.
- Each brand reads up to 100 popular series and movies from TMDb discover, and a network reads 200 films. TMDb's weekly trending lists are read whole, about 10,000 series and 10,000 movies, once for every brand: a smaller brand's titles sit far down them. The relay answers them in 20 blocks of 500 titles each, cut down to what matching needs, four at a time. A list counts only when every block arrives, as a gap would move later titles up; otherwise each brand keeps its trending titles from the last refresh, and Vivid reads only the trending lists again when Home or a brand page next loads an hour or more later (at most once an hour after that) and matches them with the saved library, instead of waiting a day for a full refresh. Series come from the network or company, studio films from the company, and network films from the US streaming lists, which are the most complete. Stan only streams in Australia, so its films come from the Australian list. BritBox mostly streams BBC and ITV series, and TMDb credits it with only a handful of its own, so its series come from its streaming list as well. A service with several plans counts all of them (Peacock Premium and Premium Plus; Paramount+ Essential and Premium), BritBox and AMC+ count their Amazon, Apple TV and Roku channels where TMDb lists them, and AMC also counts its own app. Sony includes its Columbia Pictures, Sony Pictures Animation and TriStar labels, Walt Disney Pictures includes Walt Disney Animation Studios, DreamWorks includes DreamWorks Pictures, Warner Bros. includes Warner Bros. Animation and Warner Bros. Television, DC includes DC Films and DC Entertainment, and 20th Century Studios includes 20th Century Fox. Only titles in the library are kept, matched by TMDb ID when the server lists one and otherwise by exact title and year; Silo list results don't currently include TMDb IDs. A brand needs six matches to appear.
- All in Your Library has no cap. It holds every library title the server tags with the brand, plus every TMDb match the server doesn't tag, and the match counts and automatic picks count the same titles. Silo sends each title's networks and studios with the library list, and Emby and Jellyfin keep a series' network with its studios, so a network counts both and a studio counts studios only. Each brand has a fixed list of names, matched whole and ignoring case, accents and spaces around them, so Disney Channel never counts as Disney+. The list is TMDb's names for the brand's networks or companies, plus older names a server may still use, such as Apple TV+, Lucasfilm Ltd., DC Comics and Paramount+ with Showtime. HBO also counts HBO Max and Max, FX counts FXX, DreamWorks counts DreamWorks Pictures and its television arms as well as DreamWorks Animation, and Warner Bros. counts every name that starts with Warner Bros., such as its Animation, Television and regional studios. BritBox also counts the BBC and ITV channels, because servers tag its series with the channel that first showed them; BBC America doesn't count. Servers rarely tag films with a streaming service, so most of a network's films come from its TMDb streaming list, which is why that list is read 10 pages deep instead of five. A film the server does tag with the network's name, as a network or as a studio, counts as well. A series a network only streams still isn't included when TMDb and the server credit it to another channel.
- A brand page shows the logo, then Popular TV Shows and Popular Movies (up to 20 each, at the Home poster size), then the All in Your Library grid. A filter menu at the end of the grid's heading row shows All, Movies or TV Shows (offered when the brand has both), and has Sort and Genre submenus. Sort offers the libraries' keys (Title, Date Added, Release Date, Year, Rating, Runtime and Resolution), newest added first to begin with; choosing the current key again flips its order, and the current key shows its direction as in the libraries' Sort menu. Rating is the IMDb rating, with the TMDb rating standing in where a title has none, which on Emby and Jellyfin is the community rating. Genre narrows to one genre. The button shows the type and any genre, and is highlighted while either narrows the grid. Titles without a value for the sort go last in either order, and changing the type drops a genre none of its titles have, so the grid never empties. The choice lasts while the page is open. The menu is native, in the library pill style, and its heading row is a focus section, so Down from any rail card lands on the filter and Down again reaches the grid. The rails use the same reusable collection row as Home. The grid's first row is built up front so Down from the filter always reaches it; the rest is the For You and library grid, which builds rows as they near the screen, prefetches nearby artwork and redraws only the row that changes. The page only redraws when that brand's own rows change, not while other brands load or refresh, and the daily library read and TMDb matching run off the main thread. Both rows lead with the brand's titles in this week's TMDb trending list, in trending order, and fill the rest from its popular titles: network rows from titles with episodes aired or a release in the past 12 months, falling back to all-time popular when fewer than six match, and studio rows from their all-time most popular, because studios release few films a year. TMDb's trending list is one worldwide list with no network, studio or region filter, so a trending title counts for a brand when it's one of the brand's library titles (anything All in Your Library lists, or a title from its past-12-months list). Some brands have fewer than 20 library titles trending anywhere in a given week, such as A24's series or Studio Ghibli, and their rows fill up from popular. The rows leave out talk shows (TMDb's Talk genre) and anime (Japanese animation, by original language or country of origin), except on Studio Ghibli; All in Your Library still lists them. Rows with fewer than six matches are hidden.
- Settings → General → Studios & Networks has a Show on Home toggle, a live preview and pick grids with match counts. Hold Select on a preview tile to pick it up. It keeps the normal card focus look while every preview tile wobbles and the others dim slightly, and focus stays on it, so left and right move it and centre places it. Press Play/Pause to remove it instead; Menu leaves arrange mode. The wobble honours Reduce Motion. Six to 20 brands can be picked; the preview shows six at a time and scrolls sideways, keeping a picked-up tile in view as it moves. Tiles that can't be picked (20 are already chosen, or a brand has fewer than six matches) are dimmed but still take focus and do nothing when selected, so every pick grid stays reachable even when none of its brands can be chosen. The pick grids aren't lazy, so Down from the networks always reaches the studio tiles instead of skipping to Reset to Automatic, and each row aligns at the top. Picks default to the 20 brands with the most matches; a chosen set is kept as chosen, topped up only when it drops below six, saved per server and profile and synced through the encrypted private iCloud vault.
- The first load starts while Vivid prepares Home, and TMDb's lists download while the library is read, with up to 12 TMDb requests in flight plus four trending blocks. Movies and series are read side by side. For each, the first page gives its size and the rest load four pages at a time, so up to eight library requests run at once; a server that sends smaller pages than asked, or no total, has that type read one page at a time. After a sign-in or profile setup, Home waits up to two minutes for it, so the row is there when Home first appears; a failure never holds Home. Switching to a saved profile doesn't wait. Results and the library index are cached on disk per server and profile. Home shows the saved row instantly and refreshes in the background once a day, keeping the saved row while it works. Watched ticks on brand pages come from that saved data, so they can lag by up to a day. Silo signs artwork URLs for a few hours, far less than the saved data lasts, so shortly before the first saved poster URL expires (checked when Home or a brand page appears, including after the app has been left open) Vivid quietly re-reads the library and swaps fresh poster URLs into the saved results without matching with TMDb again. That read also brings in titles the server has tagged since, so on Silo new additions reach All in Your Library within a few hours instead of a day. The daily refresh doesn't reuse a library index whose posters have expired, or one saved before server tags were read. When the saved posters have expired or are about to by the daily refresh, they take the fresh library read's links as soon as it finishes, before TMDb is matched, so they don't stay blurred if matching is slow or fails. If the library read itself fails, the posters keep their links until a visit after the 15-minute cooldown reads the library again. Results matched by an earlier version are matched again on the next load, keeping the saved row while it works. Emby and Jellyfin poster URLs don't expire, so they skip this. Account removal deletes the profile's cached files. The first load reads the whole library once, which can take a minute or two on a large library (Home stops waiting for it after two minutes); the settings page shows live progress and asks to keep Vivid open.
- Search uses the saved library index too, matching titles on the device with typos, spacing and punctuation allowed, so the library is read even when Show on Home is off. Titles brand matching can't key, with neither a TMDb ID nor a year or whose keys another title already holds (such as a second copy of a film), are kept in it for Search. See [Search matching](server-connections.md#library-index).

Home applies the shared six-section limit to Jellyfin as well as the other providers. Emby and Jellyfin each offer their own Combine Continue Watching and Next Up option. When enabled, Spotlight’s Continue Watching choice includes both sources, interleaved within the ten-item limit; the Home row retains resume ordering and removes duplicate Next Up items.

## Collections row

- Home pins a Collections row below the last server row. Whenever it shows, it is the bottom row, and it sits outside the six-row limit. It holds up to 20 collections as posters at Home's Large size with the Home caption setting (the second line is the collection's title count), and selecting one opens the collection's page. Down from the last server row and Up back are native, and returning from a collection's page puts focus back on the card that opened it. The row reserves the same height as a poster row and uses the same nearby-row artwork window. It only appears on Home, and it is absent when Show on Home is off, nothing is chosen or the server has no collections. It also needs something else on Home: with every Home row hidden and no Spotlight, Home shows its empty state without it.
- The list is the server's own collections (what For You shows with Source set to Server) plus, on Silo, the profile's own personal collections (what Source set to Mine shows). Emby and Jellyfin return every box set in one request; Silo is read from each movie, series and mixed library, and a collection that appears in more than one library is shown once. The profile's own collections are read from its collections list; other profiles' shared collections are left out. A library the profile can't see (Silo answers it with Library not found) counts as having no collections. The list is cached per server and profile, so Home shows the row straight away. Home reads it again when it is 30 minutes old or a signed Silo poster is within 15 minutes of expiring, and pulling to refresh on iPhone and iPad or opening the settings page reads it at once. After a failed read it waits 5 minutes before another visit tries again, so a slow server isn't asked on every visit; pulling to refresh and the settings page still read at once. If one Silo library or the personal list fails to load, the others still show, with the previous list's collections from what failed kept. That partial list isn't cached or treated as fresh, so the next Home visit reads the server again, and editing choices meanwhile keeps the ones from that library. Collections removed from the server drop out of the row.
- Settings > General > Collections has a Show on Home toggle, a live preview of the chosen posters, the collections A to Z in carousels of 20 (on Silo, the profile's own collections come first, then the server's, each A to Z), and Reset to Automatic. Select a poster in a carousel to add it to the row or remove it; up to 20 can be chosen, and once 20 are chosen the others dim and do nothing. Hold Select on a preview poster to pick it up: every preview poster wobbles, left and right move it, centre drops it and Play/Pause removes it; Menu leaves arrange mode. The wobble honours Reduce Motion. The carousels show posters only; VoiceOver reads each name. They are built up front so Down always reaches the next carousel and then Reset, and each builds the posters near the screen as it scrolls. Posters load for the carousels around focus and stay loaded once shown, so scrolling back never blanks a row. Automatic shows the 20 server collections with the most titles, ties A to Z, with Silo's smart collections (which have no count) last. The profile's own collections only show when chosen. Choices are saved per server and profile and synced through the encrypted private iCloud vault.
- On Emby servers before 4.10.0.4, the user's Emby Home settings can also add Emby's own Collections row, so two rows with that name can appear. Vivid doesn't hide the server's row.

## Device metadata cache

Settings → Metadata lists Spotlight and the enabled Home rows, including Continue Watching. Each entry has a separate clear-cache button. Only enabled Home rows are retained, capped at twenty items per row; the spotlight is capped at ten. Disabling a row or receiving refreshed content removes obsolete entries and artwork references.

`TVHomeMetadataCache` stores versioned snapshots in the Apple TV cache directory, isolated by server and viewing profile. A snapshot saved in another language isn't used, because it holds row titles Vivid named itself. Cached rows, libraries and spotlight details hydrate the existing response cache before network refresh. Artwork uses the local `VividImagePipeline` cache with bounded prefetching. These are disposable caches: tvOS may reclaim them, and clearing them does not remove server media or watched history.

On iPhone, iPad and Apple TV, repeated artwork lookups reuse the last calculated server/account/profile cache key without changing its on-disk format. Home snapshot saves combine queued updates for the same account; writes already running finish normally, and reads and account deletion preserve queue ordering. No debounce delay is added, and in-memory Home updates remain immediate. These changes reduce redundant work; app performance and playback still require device verification.

Cold launch and profile/login preparation read and decode the Home snapshot asynchronously on its serial I/O queue before routing to Home. First paint still hydrates from memory and does not wait for fresh server content. Concurrent preparation shares one read; an intervening activation, clear, deletion or profile change invalidates that result. Direct callers outside these preparation paths retain the synchronous fallback so cached first paint remains available. The snapshot format, size cap and Settings clear/save controls are unchanged.

Silo, Emby and Jellyfin artwork requests on iPhone, iPad and Apple TV share an in-flight transfer only when both the full URL and account/profile scope match. Display, crop, palette and disk warming can reuse those bytes while retaining separate size-specific decodes. Signed URL queries remain part of the key. A cancelled consumer does not cancel consumers still using the transfer; cancelling the last consumer or explicitly clearing its account cancels the transfer. Completed bytes use the existing HTTP cache, and retry budgets, download limits and file-URL handling are unchanged.

Home uses a vertical `VStack` of reserved-height `VividCollectionMediaRow` rails backed by CollectionHStack, with the full metadata cache, expanded artwork cache and nearby-row warming. Row dimensions follow Home Sections visibility and the caption setting; Home posters are always Large. The six-enabled-rail limit excludes Spotlight; empty enabled rows reserve a slot. Row focus updates are isolated from the feed. The owner accepted the cleanup and native card effects on Apple TV.

The tvOS discovery Home raises the decoded artwork cache budget to 320 MiB on Apple TVs with more than 3.5 GB physical memory, or 192 MiB on smaller models, with a 600-image count limit. It warms one screenful of exact display-sized artwork for the focused row, the next three rows and the preceding row, each starting around that row's remembered card, using two concurrent requests. It restores the normal cache budget and cancels queued warming when Home disappears, the app leaves the foreground or a memory warning arrives. Existing memory-pressure and playback cache clearing remain. These are cache budgets, not measured app memory or strict process limits. Metadata and disk caching remain separate; layout and drawing still occur. The owner reported smoother navigation, but peak memory and a controlled performance comparison have not been measured for the final source.

Home hydrates its snapshot immediately, then follows the provider-specific [Home refresh policy](server-connections.md#home-refresh). Jellyfin polls every ten seconds while Home is visible and active; Silo refreshes every thirty minutes for signed artwork renewal; Emby has no periodic Home timer. Final playback-progress writes and watched-state changes also request updates. Hidden requests wait until return, and stale responses cannot replace newer state. Continue Watching advances or disappears once the server response reflects the change. Cached startup is intended to reduce waiting, but no before/after device benchmark is recorded for this revision.

## Tabs and catalogue pages

The slim glass tab bar contains Search, Home, Movies, Series, For You, Calendar and the profile/settings avatar as available. Calendar has its own show/hide eye in Settings > General > Tab Bar, outside the reorderable destinations, and when shown always sits just after For You (at the end when For You is hidden). It shares the profile setting with the iPhone and iPad Calendar tab. Dividers separate Search and Profile. Focusing a tab gives that individual control a lift. The avatar opens Settings directly. Root pages have no separate Vivid logo in the top-left corner.

Movies and Series use library sub-tabs above Sort and Filter, without the old category dropdowns or mixed Recommended/Browse landing page. Redundant leading words such as “Movies” or “TV Shows” are shortened in sub-tab labels; colliding labels retain the original library names. Each page keeps its own selection and filter scope. Movie and series queries filter content by type, including mixed libraries such as Sports; classification depends on the server's metadata.

Opening Movies or Series, or switching library sub-tabs, paints the library's saved first page straight away, then fetches that page again once and swaps the new one in behind the posters already showing. The saved page is kept in memory until the app quits or the profile changes, and later pages are fenced to the time the first page loaded, so without this refetch a newly added title stayed hidden everywhere in the grid until the sort changed or the app restarted. Coming back from a title or returning to Vivid with a grid on screen doesn't refetch, and once the grid has loaded past its first page it is left as it is, so your place in the list is kept; switching tabs, changing the sort or filter, or choosing a letter loads it fresh.

Sort, Filter and A–Z use native tvOS menus. A–Z is available whatever the sort; choosing a letter switches that library to Title A to Z, as on iPhone and iPad. Filters become available after their facets load, avoiding a partial menu that changes while open. The menu owns focus while presented and restores it to its trigger on dismissal. Up and Down between the library sub-tabs, this control row and the grid use native focus, so Siri Remote swipes and clicks behave the same: Down from the row lands on the poster below, and entering the row returns to the last control used. Entering the sub-tabs lands on the current library. Only Up to the top menu, which stays disabled while content owns focus, is a boundary handler. An earlier version disabled the grid and sub-tabs while the row had focus and moved focus manually on clicks, which left swipes stuck on the row.

For You has Watchlist, Favourites and Collections sub-tabs, with no Sort control. Watchlist and Favourites each have an independent native A–Z menu; choosing All restores the unfiltered list. Collections has no A–Z. Watchlist and Favourites also have a native Filter menu left of A–Z (Show, Original Language and Anime), and Silo Collections has one at the end of the row (Source; Emby and Jellyfin have none), as described in [App Design](app-design.md). Each sub-tab keeps its own filter and shows "Filter · n" when filters are on. Up from the Filter menu hands off to the top menu like the sub-tabs and A–Z. Up and Down between the sub-tab row and the posters use native focus, landing on the poster below and returning to the current sub-tab; only Up to the top menu is a boundary handler. A manual Down claim used to race native focus and made the grid jump to its first poster. Collections combines movie and series collections, and on Silo adds this profile's own collections to User Collections, which comes first, above the server's Collections. It paints cached per-library sections immediately, refreshes source libraries together and publishes each response as it arrives instead of waiting for the slowest library. The library list is the account's, so it can include libraries the profile hides or isn't allowed; Silo answers those with Library not found, and they count as empty. A library that fails to load leaves the others on screen, and the page only shows an error when nothing loads. Posters load for the rows around the cards on screen; a card the lazy grid drops counts as off screen, so the window follows the scroll instead of staying on rows already passed. When no card counts as on screen, as after returning from a collection, the window follows the remembered focus. Catalogue grids use seven posters across, with deliberate space above the first row for focus enlargement.

Calendar is laid out like For You, with Upcoming and Trending sub-tabs and a Calendar pill on the right where For You keeps Filter and A–Z. Upcoming shows the same episodes as on iPhone and iPad (see [App Design](app-design.md)), as cards grouped by day, three across; choosing one opens the series. Trending shows rails of series and movies coming soon with local date badges, and choosing a poster shows its date and overview. The Calendar pill opens a month view of the page's window: on Upcoming, choosing a day with a dot scrolls to it; on Trending, it shows that day's releases with Show all to go back.

Search uses seven posters across the available safe-area width, without the previous extra horizontal insets or width cap. The results sit closer to the Top Results, Movies and Series tabs.

### Siri search

A short press of the Siri Remote's Siri button anywhere in Vivid brings up Apple's dictation prompt. Say what you want to watch and Vivid opens Search with those words in the field and the results showing, on Silo, Emby and Jellyfin alike. Typing still works afterwards: the words can be edited in the search field like any other search. Holding the button still opens Siri.

tvOS never gives apps the Siri button itself, so this uses App Intents' in-app search (`ShowInAppSearchResultsIntent`, tvOS 17.2 and later): tvOS runs `SearchVividIntent` with the spoken words, and Vivid hands them to its own `vivid://search?q=` link. That link waits behind sign-in, profile selection and the background profile lock like a Top Shelf link, then Search replaces whatever screens were open, exactly as if Search had been picked in the top bar. Back returns focus to the bar. A playing video closes the way Menu closes it, so its progress is saved first. Search resets to Top Results so an earlier Movies or Series filter can't hide the title. A request with no words is ignored, so it can't close a video or the screens you're on.

Use a short tap. A hold is handled by tvOS, which starts Siri; Vivid doesn't receive it. There are no spoken phrases: an earlier build registered "Search Vivid" as an App Shortcut, but saying it to Siri on an Apple TV did nothing, so it was removed.

Validation so far: on 15 October 2026 the owner confirmed on an Apple TV 4K (3rd generation), tvOS 27.0, running a signed Release build of `83d8417`, that a short Siri press in Vivid brought up Apple's prompt and the spoken title opened Search with its results, and that "Search Vivid" spoken to Siri did nothing. The tap still worked on a Release build of `6fa8bd8`, after the phrases were removed. The `vivid://search` routing was also checked in the Apple TV 4K (3rd generation) simulator from Home, from an open Search, from a detail page, during playback and from a cold launch. The prompt and speech recognition are Apple's, and depend on Siri and Dictation being available for the Apple TV's language and region and on Settings → General → Dictation. Vivid receives only the resulting text. iPhone and iPad don't register the intent.

Library requests load seventy items at a time. Artwork retention uses a moving ten-row window that follows visible rows, falling back to current focus before visibility is known. It keeps a small look-behind and releases earlier decoded image references as scrolling advances. Visible and upcoming posters are warmed before the look-behind, with two requests at a time; queue updates can refill evicted images. Visible or focused catalogue cards always remain eligible to load, and distant cards release their image through the same image view without replacing its subtree. Lightweight item metadata remains available for navigation; this is not a fixed total app-memory limit. Seven-column catalogue grids request the next page with four rows remaining and recheck when a row becomes visible or a page arrives. Native card controls, focus styling and layout are unchanged. The owner confirmed that the installed scrolling follow-up resolved the Movies and Series focus/artwork issue.

Shared artwork loads retain a consumer for each visible image or prefetch request. Cancelling one consumer preserves work needed elsewhere; cancelling the last consumer stops the shared transfer and skips any decode still queued. Account changes continue to cancel outgoing work and prevent it from repopulating memory. The same cancellation behaviour applies on iPhone and iPad.

Decoding older saved menus drops the retired Calendar entry while preserving supported destinations. The Calendar tab described above isn't part of the saved menu: its own setting shows or hides it. This does not modify any media-server backend.

## Startup and Settings

The removed marquee, legacy Browse/Recommended landings, music tab, pinned library shortcuts and tab dropdowns no longer run their former tvOS startup prefetch/cache paths. Startup retains Home and profile warmup. Home row artwork selection stays bounded, and already-warmed candidates are skipped before queuing. Movies, Series and For You load their own current-page caches when opened.

Cold startup uses Vivid’s two-piece Glide logo, with Reduce Motion and a static-logo fallback. The pieces assemble once; when content is ready, the logo fades out before the destination fades in, following the [shared startup handoff](app-design.md#settings-and-startup). Startup routing resolves local and private-iCloud account state, but does not wait for a full network population of Home. A usable restored session can open Home, while no usable session returns to server selection instead of an old provider login. Cached Home content can then appear while refresh continues. Saved-account selection, iCloud deletion and PIN rules are described in [Server Connections](server-connections.md#saved-accounts-on-apple-tv-iphone-and-ipad).

Settings categories open separate native navigation pages with white headings and no repeated eyebrow. Section text and secondary actions use grey. Diagnostics sits directly below Metadata and lists the reports kept on the Apple TV. Send to Vivid sends them to the diagnostics relay, with a QR code as the fallback for emailing a summary; see [Diagnostics reports](app-design.md#diagnostics-reports). About follows it as a main Settings category; it contains app version/build, the source commit, privacy, licences, Acknowledgements and Contact. TMDb attribution is in Acknowledgements, without a duplicate About-page block. The Contact page shows an email QR code. Playback uses the same Intro & Credit Skipper toggle as mobile; Settings → Subtitles owns local subtitle language, behaviour and appearance. The Episodes group also offers Hide Episode Spoilers, which covers episodes after the viewer’s position on the series and season pages as described in [Playback](playback/README.md). The Audio group between Playback and Episodes holds Audio Language, Volume Boost, Boost Level (dimmed and skipped by focus while Volume Boost is off, like the custom subtitle appearance rows) and Enhanced Dialogue, as on mobile; see [Volume Boost and Enhanced Dialogue](cores/player-engine.md#volume-boost-and-enhanced-dialogue). ## Trailers and More Like This

TMDb powers trailers, the TMDb rating, Studios & Networks and movie collection rows. Requests go through Vivid's [TMDb relay](tmdb-relay.md), so there's no key to set up; Settings > Plugins > TMDb shows the connection status and TMDb's attribution. Without a signed-in account and profile, trailer rows are hidden. Detail-page requests select up to three unique YouTube trailers, preferring the main trailer and, for series, trailers for the latest season. Results are cached; the physical Apple TV opens YouTube to play them. The movie and series technical row ends with the TMDb user score after the quality badges, for titles TMDb matches and has votes for.

More Like This is independent of TMDb. Emby and Jellyfin use their native similar-items endpoints on Apple TV. Silo uses the active server/profile catalogue, filters by shared genres, studios or networks, and samples up to three hundred candidates across the matching catalogue after reading its count. Genre overlap dominates ranking, followed by production and release era. Ties use a stable title-independent order. Up to ten results are shown, excluding the current item and wrong media types. This is bounded metadata matching, not a full-library semantic recommendation engine; sparse metadata can produce few or no matches. Its posters and loading placeholders use Home’s Large poster size.
